## DEEP-DIVE 116 — The Saudi Engineering Talent Market

### The supply-demand gap

- Saudi Vision 2030 has created massive tech demand (PIF tech investments, Aramco digital, banking digitization)
- Saudi engineering schools (KFUPM, KSU, KAUST, Effat) produce ~3,000-5,000 CS graduates/year
- Demand: 10,000+ Saudi engineering openings/year
- Net: shortage. Specifically acute for senior engineers (5+ years experience)

### Who Falcon competes with for talent

**Tier 1 — The "FAANG-equivalents" in Saudi:**
- Aramco Digital (massive compensation, complex tech)
- STC (strong telco brand, stable)
- SABIC Digital (manufacturing tech, strong compensation)
- Saudi banks (strong compensation, less interesting tech)
- PIF / Tonomus / NEOM (giga-projects, very high compensation, unclear stability)

**Tier 2 — Other Saudi tech companies:**
- Other Saudi SaaS (similar pay, similar challenges)
- Saudi consulting firms (Accenture, McKinsey Digital)

**Tier 3 — Global remote opportunities:**
- US/UK companies hiring Saudi remote talent (USD compensation, time-zone challenge)
- European startups with remote-friendly policies

### Falcon's hiring proposition vs each tier

| Competitor type | Falcon's advantage | Falcon's disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Aramco/SABIC | Smaller team = bigger impact + faster career growth | Lower comp ceiling |
| STC/Mobily | Modern tech stack vs legacy telco | Less brand prestige |
| Saudi banks | More interesting tech + dynamic environment | Lower comp |
| PIF mega-projects | Real product + customers vs PowerPoint projects | Less prestige |
| Global remote | Same time zone + KSA-resident | Often lower comp than US$ remote |

### The "Saudization" reality

- Saudi labor law requires hiring Saudi nationals (% quotas vary by sector)
- For tech: typically 30%+ Saudi national requirement
- Saudi nationals get preference + higher comp expectations
- Non-Saudi expats are still essential for senior + niche roles

Falcon's hiring mix must balance:
- Saudi national requirements (regulatory)
- Senior expat hires (knowledge import)
- Junior Saudi hires (long-term capability building)

## DEEP-DIVE 117 — Compensation Strategy

### Saudi tech compensation benchmarks (rough, 2026)

| Role | Junior (0-3 yrs) | Mid (3-7 yrs) | Senior (7+ yrs) | Staff/Principal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Software Engineer (general) | 15-25k SAR/mo | 25-45k | 45-70k | 70k+ |
| DevOps / SRE | 18-28k | 30-50k | 50-80k | 80k+ |
| Data Scientist / ML Engineer | 20-30k | 35-55k | 55-85k | 85k+ |
| Engineering Manager | n/a | 40-55k | 55-90k | 90k+ |
| Sales (AE) | 12-20k base + variable | 20-40k base + variable | 40-70k base + variable | OTE 100-200k+ |
| CSM | 15-25k | 25-40k | 40-60k | n/a |

Note: Saudi nationals typically command 20-40% premium for equivalent roles due to scarcity + regulatory demand.

### Falcon's compensation philosophy options

**Option A — Market-leading**
- Pay top of market in every role
- Attract best talent
- Higher burn rate
- Suited for well-funded growth companies

**Option B — Market-competitive**
- Pay 75th percentile of market
- Attract strong (not elite) talent
- Sustainable burn
- Most common for scale-stage

**Option C — Market-lagging + equity-heavy**
- Pay 50th percentile cash
- Heavy equity grants
- Attract believers + risk-takers
- Suited for early-stage

**Recommendation for Falcon:** Option B (market-competitive cash) + meaningful equity. Saudi market values cash; equity is upside but not primary attractor in Saudi culture (vs US tech where equity dominates).

### Compensation components

| Component | Typical % of total comp |
|---|---|
| Base salary | 60-80% |
| Performance bonus | 10-20% (annual) |
| Equity (RSU / options) | 10-25% (vesting over 4 yrs) |
| Benefits (medical, housing, transport) | 5-10% |
| Long-term incentives (executives) | varies |

### Special considerations

- **Housing allowance** — common in Saudi expat packages
- **Children's school fees** — significant expat benefit
- **Annual home-leave ticket** — for expats
- **End-of-service gratuity** — mandatory per Saudi labor law
- **Saudi national savings boost** — Saudi nationals can get government subsidies for housing/loans

## DEEP-DIVE 118 — Recruiting Channels + Process

### Where Falcon should source candidates

**1. LinkedIn (highest yield)**
- Saudi tech talent is heavily on LinkedIn
- Sourcing reps + targeted ads
- Especially for senior + specialized roles

**2. University relationships**
- KFUPM, KSU, KAUST, Effat
- Internship programs (capture talent early)
- Career fairs + sponsored events

**3. Saudi tech communities**
- Meetups (Riyadh, Jeddah, Khobar)
- WhatsApp groups (where Saudi engineers actually communicate)
- Tech conferences (Beyond, Tech-tonic, GITEX KSA)

**4. Employee referrals**
- Best yield + cultural fit
- Referral bonuses (5-10k SAR per successful hire)

**5. Specialized recruiters**
- Saudi tech recruiting firms (Robert Half KSA, Hays Saudi, etc.)
- Pay 20-25% of first-year salary
- Use sparingly — expensive

**6. Direct from competitors**
- Source from Saudi banks (digital teams), other Saudi tech
- Aramco Digital + STC produce strong talent that sometimes wants change
- Be respectful of relationships

### Interview process (recommended)

**Step 1 — Recruiter screen** (30 min)
- Cultural fit + role expectations + comp alignment

**Step 2 — Hiring manager** (45 min)
- Past projects + technical depth assessment
- Mutual fit signals

**Step 3 — Technical assessment**
- Take-home for engineers (4-8 hours work)
- Live coding interviews ARE controversial — Saudi candidates often less comfortable with whiteboard
- System design interviews for senior+

**Step 4 — Onsite/virtual loop** (3-5 interviews)
- Coding deep-dive
- System design
- Domain knowledge
- Behavioral / culture-fit

**Step 5 — Final + offer**
- Founder/CEO if seniorlevel
- Reference checks
- Offer + negotiation

### Process pitfalls in Saudi

- **Speed matters** — slow process = lose to faster competitors
- **Cultural sensitivity** — interview questions about religion, gender, family are inappropriate
- **Salary negotiation expectations** — direct/explicit; lowball offers waste everyone's time
- **Decision speed** — Saudi candidates expect offers within 1-2 weeks of final interview

## DEEP-DIVE 119 — Retention Strategy

### Why people leave (and how to prevent)

**Reason 1 — Compensation gap to market**
- Mitigation: annual market reviews + corrections + retention bonuses for top performers
- Specific Saudi addition: factor in housing inflation (significant in Riyadh)

**Reason 2 — Career growth stagnation**
- Mitigation: clear career ladder, regular promotion cycles, skip-level mentorship
- Particularly important for ambitious Saudi nationals (Vision 2030 = ambition culture)

**Reason 3 — Manager quality issues**
- Mitigation: manager training, regular 360 reviews, swift action on bad managers
- A bad manager will lose 3-5 reports within 12 months

**Reason 4 — Boring work**
- Mitigation: rotation opportunities, internal hackathons, 20% time for innovation
- Particularly for senior engineers — they want hard problems

**Reason 5 — Cultural friction**
- Mitigation: inclusive culture, respect Saudi customs (prayer times, Ramadan), modern but rooted
- This is hard in mixed Saudi/expat teams — invest in management culture work

**Reason 6 — Better offer from FAANG-equivalent**
- Mitigation: counter-offer policy (be willing to match for top performers)
- Cap counter-offers (if you have to keep counter-offering, person was already mentally out)

### Engagement signals to monitor

- Quarterly engagement surveys (e.g., Officevibe, CultureAmp)
- Glassdoor / public review sites
- 1:1 candor (do reports feel safe to speak truth?)
- Voluntary departure rate (industry average ~10-15%/year; target <10%)
- Time-in-role distribution (concentration of <1-year tenures = warning)

### Retention through compensation

- Annual reviews + adjustments
- Promotion-based raises (10-15% typical for promotion)
- Spot bonuses for exceptional work
- Equity refreshes for long-tenure employees

### Retention through career growth

- Clear ladder: IC track + management track
- Regular promotion cycles (semi-annual)
- Internal mobility (engineer → product engineer → product manager possible)
- External visibility (speaking opportunities, open source contributions)

### Retention through culture

- Saudi-specific: respect prayer times, Friday off, Ramadan accommodations
- Modern-specific: hybrid work, professional development budgets, parental leave
- Falcon-specific: tie work to platform impact (every engineer sees their code affect real Saudi businesses)
